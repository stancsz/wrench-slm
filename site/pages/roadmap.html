The mission / More people, more possibilities

Affordable AI
for the rest of us.

You should be able to build useful things with AI even if your GPU has less than 8 GB of memory. The goal is free-to-use software that makes the hardware you already own more useful.

Wrench starts with everyday developer work because a small, checkable task gives us a clear way to prove usefulness. Read a setting. Find the relevant lines. Return trustworthy evidence. Keep larger models available for the parts that need them, and measure the whole cost.

Spend the good tokens on the hard parts. The longer-term cost ambition is cloud-model spending below 0.5% of a cloud baseline for the same successfully completed workload. This is distinct from the current 95% net frontier-token reduction target. Neither is an achieved general result; local overhead still belongs in the total cost.

Open source. Open weights. Open datasets.

Affordability should come with the freedom to build. We will open-source the code and make the model weights and datasets available, so you can study, modify, reproduce, and contribute improvements. The open release is in preparation; specific licenses and download links will accompany it.

First audience: less than 8 GB

We are designing for people with modest GPUs, rather than treating an expensive hardware upgrade as the entry ticket. The immediate work is making one bounded workflow useful, reliable, and demonstrably cheaper.

Acceptance still needed: a named device with less than 8 GB of GPU memory, a reproducible task, verified correct results, measured peak memory, and matched end-to-end cost and latency. Running a no-model tool example does not prove that a model fits this hardware budget.

Next milestone: less than 2 GB

Make a similar useful workflow work well with less than 2 GB of GPU memory. Success means a task that completes correctly at an acceptable speed while leaving the device usable. A tiny model file alone is not enough.

Older phones are a related target: devices people already have, often sitting unused. A phone’s shared memory, available runtime, battery, and heat limits need their own measurements. “Less than 2 GB GPU memory” is not a universal phone specification.

The longer-term vision: lend your FLOPs

A FLOP is a floating-point operation, one unit of computation. The human idea is simpler: let a friend borrow some computing power you are not using.

Imagine choosing when your spare device is available, what it may process, and when to stop sharing. That could let a small group make better use of hardware they already own.

This is a product vision, not an available network or a promise that small devices can already run one large model together. Shared compute needs a separate design for consent, private data, task verification, network overhead, and stopping a job.

How we will tell whether we are getting there

  • Useful: the same real task completes correctly.
  • Affordable: count total cloud and local cost, including retries.
  • Small enough: measure runtime memory on the named device.
  • Comfortable to run: measure latency and sustained device impact.
  • Trustworthy: permissions stay narrow and results can be checked.

The current worker, next hardware milestone, and future sharing idea have different levels of maturity. The evidence page keeps that distinction visible.
