The integration contract in plain English

  1. The client sends a bounded request.
  2. Wrench proposes an allowed action or declines.
  3. An independent verifier checks the proposal and the permitted read or review operation runs.
  4. The workflow returns a final answer tied to the verified result, or returns the original request to its configured fallback.

This is a two-state protocol, with a proposal followed by a verified result and final answer. Repeated, open-ended autonomous tool loops are outside its scope.

Before trying your own workflow

  • Run the no-model example first.
  • Choose a small, non-sensitive workspace and an explicitly allowed task.
  • Record the client version, server configuration, and fallback choice.
  • Check the final answer, not just whether the endpoint returns HTTP 200.
  • Count failures, retries, local overhead, and any paid fallback usage.

Full client experiments may need local model artifacts and provider configuration. The free, no-key example does not establish the requirements for those experiments. We are keeping client setup as a controlled developer evaluation until a reproducible public distribution is ready.

Other clients and hardware

Additional clients, stock Ollama/vLLM/GGUF adapters, and separate hardware-portability work are outside the active goal. Support should follow demonstrated user demand and verified workflows.
